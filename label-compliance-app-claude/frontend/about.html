About us

Small team. Serious about labels.

We’re NEXORA — six students from Jalpaiguri Government Engineering College (JGEC) building open-source tooling for people who have to get packaging right. MetrIQ started from a simple frustration: checking a label against Indian legal metrology rules meant juggling PDFs of regulations, a magnifying glass, and guesswork. We wanted one tool that reads the pack and points at the exact rule — so inspectors, brands, and small businesses can move faster without trusting a black box.

What we believe

Principles, printed in

Fail loudly, not confidently

When the OCR is unsure, we say so. Every check is either pass, fail, incomplete, or explicitly conditional — never a silent guess dressed up as a verdict.

Open source, always

No paid APIs, no lock-in, no data leaving your machine. Anyone can read the rule engine, audit it, and extend it to a new product category.

Cite everything

A verdict is only useful if you can defend it. Each check names the rule it comes from — Rule 6(1)(a), FSS Reg. 5, Drugs Rules 96 — so you can verify in the source.

Legal basis

The rules we check against

Every product type is judged against the baseline declarations of Rule 6, LMPC Rules 2011 — plus the sector-specific regulations that apply.

Rule 6, LMPC Rules 2011Baseline declarations for every pre-packaged commodity
Rule 27, LMPC Rules 2011Country of origin & importer details on imported packages
FSS (Packaging & Labelling) Regs., 2020Food: FSSAI license, ingredients, allergens, veg mark
Cosmetics Rules, 2020Cosmetics: license, INCI list, batch, use-before period
Drugs & Cosmetics Rules, 1945Drugs: license no., batch no., expiry date
BIS Compulsory RegistrationElectronics: BIS/ISI mark reference (informational)
